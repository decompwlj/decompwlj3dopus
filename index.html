Weight × level + jump, 5000 sequences

Every term of a strictly increasing sequence is written a(n) = k(n)·L(n) + d(n): the jump d = a(n+1) − a(n), the weight k the least divisor of a − d above d, the level L = (a − d)/k. 10⁵ terms per sequence. Open a plate to explore it in three dimensions; the A-number under it opens the sequence on the OEIS.

Each plate is the weight–level plane on a square with equal aspect: log k / log A across, log L / log A up, A the largest term shown. Every sequence then shares one frame. Points lie below the dotted edge kL = A, and level-classified terms (k > L) fall below the dashed diagonal k = L. Blue is weight (k ≤ L, ties included), orange is level.

Definition

For a strictly increasing sequence a(1) < a(2) < …
jump d(n) = a(n+1) − a(n)
ℓ(n) = a(n) − d(n) when that exceeds d(n), else 0
weight k(n) = least divisor of ℓ(n) greater than d(n)
level L(n) = ℓ(n) / k(n)
a(n) = k(n) · L(n) + d(n)

Axes: log₁₀ k (weight), log₁₀ L (level), log₁₀ d (jump), one unit per decade. Every decomposable term lies on the hyperbola k·L = ℓ. The term is level-classified when k > L, weight-classified otherwise — readable here as which side of the k = L plane it falls on. Non-decomposable terms carry no point.
