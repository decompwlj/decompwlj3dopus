    The congruence lock

    For each prime r, how much more often r divides ℓ than it would at random: r·Pr[r | ℓ], over the records with d < r. 1 is chance; r/(r − 2) is the Hardy–Littlewood value for consecutive primes (ℓ ≡ 0 mod r is one admissible class in r − 2). Whiskers: ±2σ.

    Method, and what the Lab cannot tell you
    • The decomposition. d = a(n+1) − a(n), ℓ = a − d when a > 2d; the weight k is the least divisor of ℓ above d and the level L = ℓ/k; a term is in the level class when k > L (ties count as weight). The sequences' own data are the published files of decompwlj 3D, checked here against their SHA-256 fingerprints.
    • The control. For each record, keep d and c = ℓ mod M; draw ℓ′ uniformly among the integers ≡ c (mod M) in [max(d + 1, ⌈0.75 ℓ⌉), ⌊1.25 ℓ⌋]; decompose ℓ′ with the same jump. The density, the jumps and the residues mod M are the sequence's own; everything else is random. Seeds are fixed (FNV-1a of the sequence id, M and the replicate), so every figure is reproducible.
    • Frozen records. When ℓ is small next to M, its class has few members in the window and the control barely moves it. The ladder table gives the share of records with fewer than 16 members; where it is large, the Δ at that M is understated, not explained.
    • Records. The control runs on a random subsample of the decomposable records (never a fixed stride: strides alias with the arithmetic). The plates show that subsample for both panels, so their densities match.
    • σ. The whiskers use the larger of the binomial bound √(p(1 − p)/N) on the control's share and the spread of its replicates. A difference of 2σ in one of 7 steps is not by itself a discovery.
    • The kernel. Exact below 2^53: trial division, deterministic Miller–Rabin, Pollard–Brent rho, in doubles with exact products. Checked against PARI/GP 2.15.4 and against the published rows.
    • What it is not. A Δ that survives M = 30030 says that something beyond the residues mod 30030 shapes the decomposition; it does not say what. For the primes the remaining step is the Hardy–Littlewood local factor of every larger prime r, which no finite M keeps.
